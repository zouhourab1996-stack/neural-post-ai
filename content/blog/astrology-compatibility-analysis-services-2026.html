Most compatibility reports reduce two people to a single percentage — "you are 78% compatible" — which is astrology as a personality quiz, not a chart analysis. Real compatibility work compares two full natal charts (synastry) and looks at the relationship as its own entity (composite chart). This guide explains what to expect and which services are worth your money.

A compatibility reading worth paying for should cover: how each person's planets aspect the other's, where needs complement and where they clash, timing of relationship transits, and concrete suggestions — not just a score.

What to look for in a compatibility service

1. Both birth times are required

Without exact birth times for both people, you cannot calculate house overlays — where one person's planets fall in the other person's houses. That is where most of the useful information lives.

2. It explains the "why," not just the "what"

Good analysis says "your Mars squares their Saturn, so action meets resistance here" — not just "you argue about money." Look for interpretations that name the planets and angles involved.

3. It covers challenges, not only harmony

Every relationship has friction. A report that only lists positive connections is either incomplete or trying to sell you something.

4. It includes timing

Current transits to both charts explain why a relationship feels easy or difficult right now. A static compatibility score ignores the fact that skies change.

Reading synastry without over-reading it

Synastry works by overlaying one chart on the other. The patterns worth noticing are usually the ones that show up more than once, not a single dramatic aspect.

Aspects that tend to matter most

  • Moon contacts — how you each handle emotion and whether you feel at ease. A Moon-Moon trine usually reads as easy familiarity; a Moon-Moon square as two different emotional tempos.
  • Venus and Mars contacts — attraction and how each of you pursues it. These describe chemistry more than longevity.
  • Mercury contacts — whether you actually understand each other in conversation. Under-rated, and often what couples argue about when the other aspects look good.
  • Saturn contacts — structure, duty, and the places where the relationship asks for work. Often described as "heavy," but also the aspect most associated with staying power.

House overlays

Beyond planet-to-planet aspects, look at where one person's planets land in the other person's houses. If your partner's Venus falls in your 10th house, the relationship is visible in your public or working life; if it falls in your 4th, it is felt at home. These overlays only work with accurate birth times for both people, which is why a service that skips the time cannot give you this layer at all.

Timing: why a good relationship has hard seasons

Current transits hit both charts. A Saturn transit to one person's Venus, or a Venus retrograde touching a composite angle, can make a stable relationship feel strained for weeks without anything being "wrong." A report that notes upcoming transits gives you context; a static compatibility score cannot.

Frequently asked questions

Can compatibility analysis predict whether a relationship will last?

No. It shows patterns, tensions, and strengths — but whether a relationship lasts depends on the people in it, not the stars.

Do both people need to believe in astrology?

No. One person can use the reading to understand the other better, even if the other is skeptical.

What if our birth times are unknown?

You can still compare planet-sign placements, but house-based analysis and precise timing will be limited.

Is a composite chart the same as synastry?

No. Synastry compares two charts; a composite chart blends them into one. They answer different questions and are best used together.

Bottom line

Skip the percentage-based compatibility quizzes. Pay for a service that asks for both full birth datasets, shows you the actual synastry and composite charts, explains the aspects in plain language, and treats the relationship as complex — not as a single number.

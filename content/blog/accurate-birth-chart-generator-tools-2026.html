A birth chart is deterministic — two competent calculators given the same date, time, and place must produce the same rising sign and house cusps. That makes it one of the easiest astrology tools to verify. This guide shows you how to check whether a generator is computing from real ephemeris data or just approximating it.

What "accurate" means in a chart generator

An accurate generator computes planetary positions from astronomical ephemeris tables, then places them against your local horizon at the moment of birth. An inaccurate one rounds signs by date, ignores your birth city, or uses a fixed timezone — errors that shift your Ascendant by a whole sign.

The two-minute verification test

  1. Enter your data once. Date, exact time, and city — never just the country.
  2. Read the Ascendant. Write it down with the degree (e.g. "Taurus 12°", not just "Taurus").
  3. Run the same data in a second, independent generator. If the two Ascendants differ by more than about one degree, one of them is handling your birth time, coordinates, or house system incorrectly.
  4. Check the Moon sign against a known full/new moon date. Your Moon sign can be cross-checked against the lunar phase on your birth date.
Why degrees matter: If your Ascendant is at 0° or 29° of a sign, a five-minute error in your birth time flips it to the neighbouring sign. Use the exact time from your birth record, not a rounded estimate, and re-run the chart if the degree sits near a cusp.

Common errors generators make

  • Ignoring historical timezones. Many regions changed their UTC offset or DST rules; a generator that applies today's rules to a 1975 birth will be wrong.
  • Defaulting to UTC. If the tool does not convert to local birth time, every house and the Ascendant will be off.
  • No house system label. If the tool shows houses but never says whether it used Placidus or Whole Sign, you cannot compare it with anything else.
  • Sun-sign tables for planet positions. Fast-moving bodies — the Moon especially — cannot be derived from a date table alone.

Free vs. paid generators

Free web generators that use a recognised ephemeris library produce charts just as accurate as paid software. Paid tools buy convenience: saved charts, printable reports, transit calendars, and batch calculations — not better astronomy. Pay for features, never for "more accurate" planets.

Frequently asked questions

Can I trust a generator that only asks for my birth date?

Only for planet-sign placements, and even then not for the Moon. Without a time and place, it cannot compute houses or the Ascendant.

Why do two sites give me different rising signs?

Usually a house-system or timezone difference. Check the degree — if both put you at 29° of a sign, a minute of time decides it, and your birth record's exact time settles it.

Which house system should I use?

Placidus is the common default in Western astrology; Whole Sign is simpler and used widely in traditional practice. Pick one and stay consistent so readings stay comparable.

Does the generator need my current city?

No — it needs your birth city. Current location only matters for relocation charts, which are a separate calculation.

Bottom line

A trustworthy generator names its ephemeris, resolves your birth city to coordinates, handles historical timezones and DST, and tells you which house system and orbs it used. Run your data through two independent tools — if they agree, you have a chart you can actually read.

How the sketch is actually produced

Every service in this category works the same way at the mechanical level: you fill in a form, a person produces a portrait, and you receive a digital file. Sellers only disagree about where the image comes from. Some describe an intuitive impression. Some describe a psychic impression of a person they believe you will meet. Some say a trained artist interprets details you supplied. None of them publish a method you can verify, and none can be independently checked — which is exactly why the price, the refund window and the sample gallery matter more than the explanation.

What you can verify is output. Look at published samples before you pay. If a gallery shows one house style across dozens of supposedly different people, you are buying that house style, not a reading.

What buyers expected versus what they got

Read enough reviews of these services and a clear pattern appears. Buyers who describe the experience positively usually wanted a piece of art — something decorative, personal, a good talking point. Buyers who describe it negatively usually wanted identification: a face they could match to a real person. The service does not reliably deliver the second thing, and the marketing frequently implies that it does.

That gap is the whole story of this category. The same $29.95 purchase is a charming keepsake to one person and a disappointing non-answer to another, and the only difference is what each walked in expecting.

Refunds, guarantees and the fine print that matters

Digital delivery complicates refunds. Once a file has been sent, a seller cannot take it back, so most policies attach conditions: a limited window, a requirement that you have not downloaded the file, or a discretion clause. Read three things before paying: how many days the window runs, whether the clock starts at purchase or at delivery, and whether you absorb any processing fee.

A 30-day window starting at delivery is reasonable. A 14-day window starting at purchase is tighter than it looks when turnaround is itself up to a week. And any policy that makes a refund conditional on the seller agreeing the sketch was “inaccurate” is not really a guarantee — there is no shared standard for accuracy to appeal to.

Who actually enjoys these

How to spot a templated gallery

One more check before paying, because it takes thirty seconds and it is the most reliable signal in this category. Open the seller's samples and compare faces across three or four different orders. If the bone structure, the nose, the hairline and the shading style are near-identical and only the hair colour and background change, you have found a template with variation applied — not a portrait produced from your details.

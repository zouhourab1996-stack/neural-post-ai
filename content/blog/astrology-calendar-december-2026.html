Saturn stations direct — December 10

Saturn has been retrograde since 26 July 2026 (19:57 UTC, at 14°44′ Aries) and turns direct on 10 December at roughly 23:30 UTC, at 7°55′ Aries. Station days — the day or two when the planet appears to stand still — are when its themes are most insistent: boundaries, obligations, and the feeling that a shortcut has stopped working.

Saturn does not finish cleanly at the station. Its post-retrograde shadow runs until it passes 14°44′ Aries again in March 2027, so the work begun in the retrograde is reviewed rather than closed. If you are inside a Saturn return, this is the point where the structural decisions made since July start showing results — see our Saturn return in Aries guide for whether you are in it.

Neptune direct, Jupiter retrograde — December 12

Two outer planets station within hours of each other. Neptune turns direct at about 22:17 UTC on 12 December at 1°36′ Aries, ending a retrograde that began on 7 July at 4°25′ Aries — a long inward passage through the fog, which is why clarity about a plan or a person often arrives in the days right after a Neptune station rather than during it.

Then, at 00:57 UTC on 13 December (19:57 Eastern on the 12th), Jupiter stations retrograde at 27°01′ Leo. It stays retrograde until 13 April 2027, turning direct at 16°59′ Leo. Jupiter retrograde is not the communications-chaos event that Mercury retrograde is: it turns the growth function inward, which makes it a review period for questions of belief, direction and expansion. Its pre-retrograde shadow began on 17 September 2026, so themes surfacing now have probably been circling since autumn. Our Jupiter retrograde 2026 guide covers the whole arc.

New moon in Sagittarius — December 8

The new moon falls at 16°56′ Sagittarius, at 19:52 Eastern on 8 December (00:52 UTC on the 9th). New moons are starting points rather than harvest moments: the traditional practice is to set intentions in the first day or two after the exact moment, while the moon is still dark and waxing.

This one sits four days before Jupiter — Sagittarius's ruler — stations retrograde, which is an unusual pairing: the month's fresh start lands inside a review. In practice that favours revisiting something you already began over launching something brand new, and it pairs well with the Geminid peak five days later.

Geminid meteor shower — night of December 13–14

The Geminids are the strongest regular shower of the year, running from about 4 December to 20 December and peaking on the night of 13–14 December. Rates at peak reach 100–150 meteors per hour from a genuinely dark site, with the radiant in Gemini near the bright star Castor, highest around 2 a.m. local time. The parent body is the asteroid 3200 Phaethon, not a comet — which is why the Geminids are slow, bright and often coloured.

2026 is a good year for it. The peak falls five days after the new moon, so the moon is a thin waxing crescent of about 20–25% illumination that sets by roughly 10 p.m. local time, leaving the best hours genuinely dark. The International Meteor Organization puts maximum at approximately 14:00 UTC on 14 December, which is daytime for the Americas — so the practical window is the pre-dawn hours of the 14th, when the radiant is highest, with the night of 14–15 December still productive.

No equipment is needed: lie back, face the darkest part of your sky, and give your eyes 20 minutes to adapt. If you want the same treatment for the autumn showers, see our Leonid, Orionid and Taurid guides.

Solstice — Sun enters Capricorn, December 21

The December solstice falls at 20:50 UTC (15:50 Eastern) on 21 December 2026, marking the Sun's ingress into Capricorn and the shortest day in the northern hemisphere. Astrologically it is the year's pivot from expansion to consolidation: the start of Capricorn season, which runs until the Sun enters Aquarius on 20 January 2027.

Full moon in Cancer — December 23

The month closes with a full moon at 2°13′ Cancer, exact at 20:28 Eastern on 23 December (01:28 UTC on the 24th). It is the third consecutive supermoon of the season and carries the traditional name Cold Moon. Full moons are completion points — the part of the cycle where something started six months ago, at the corresponding Cancer new moon, becomes visible.

The Cancer–Capricorn axis is the domestic-versus-public one: home, family and emotional security on one side; career, structure and public standing on the other. With the solstice two days earlier, this full moon lands inside the first days of Capricorn season, which puts that balance in unusually sharp focus to end the year.

What are the main astrology events in December 2026?

Saturn stations direct on 10 December, Neptune turns direct and Jupiter turns retrograde on 12–13 December, the new moon in Sagittarius falls on 8 December, the Geminid meteor shower peaks on the night of 13–14 December, the solstice is on 21 December and the full moon in Cancer is on 23 December.

When does Jupiter go retrograde in 2026?

Jupiter stations retrograde at 27°01′ Leo at 00:57 UTC on 13 December 2026 — 19:57 US Eastern on 12 December — and turns direct at 16°59′ Leo on 13 April 2027. Its pre-retrograde shadow began on 17 September 2026.

When is the full moon in December 2026?

The December full moon is at 2°13′ Cancer, exact at 01:28 UTC on 24 December 2026 — 20:28 US Eastern on 23 December. It is the final of three consecutive supermoons and is traditionally called the Cold Moon.

When is the best time to watch the Geminids in 2026?

The pre-dawn hours of 14 December, when the radiant in Gemini is highest. The shower's formal maximum is around 14:00 UTC on 14 December, which is daytime in the Americas, but activity is strong all through the night of 13–14 December and the moon sets by about 10 p.m., leaving dark skies.

Is Mercury retrograde in December 2026?

No. Mercury is direct throughout December 2026, moving into Sagittarius on 6 December and into Capricorn on 25 December. The retrograde activity that month comes from Jupiter, which stations retrograde on 13 December.

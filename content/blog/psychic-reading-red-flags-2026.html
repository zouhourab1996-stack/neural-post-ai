Almost every bad psychic reading follows one of a small number of scripts. They are not subtle once you have seen them written down, and none of them depend on whether the reader believes what they are saying. Learn the nine patterns and you will spot them in a gig description before you spend a cent.

The one-sentence version: if a reading manufactures a problem it can then solve for money, you are not buying a reading. You are being sold to.

The nine red flags

1. The curse, block, or hex

The oldest script in the book: something is blocking you, someone has sent you negative energy, and the reader can clear it — for an additional fee that is almost always larger than the reading itself. This is the single most common complaint in spiritual marketplaces, and it is the one to walk away from fastest.

2. Fear-based predictions

A reading that centres on illness, accident, or loss, then offers a paid remedy, is using fear as a closing technique. Reputable readers decline to predict death, illness, or legal outcomes.

3. Guaranteed outcomes

"Your ex will return in 21 days." Nobody can guarantee that, and the specificity is the tell — vague hope is free, whereas a dated promise is what makes people pay for a follow-up.

4. Pressure to act now

Countdown timers on an upsell, "the energy window closes tonight", "if you wait the work will not hold". Urgency exists to stop you thinking, not because time matters.

5. The upsell ladder

Read the package tiers. If Standard and Premium add "cleansing", "protection", "block removal" or "extra energy work" rather than more reading time, the reading is a lead magnet for the upsell.

6. Payment off-platform

Any suggestion of WhatsApp, direct bank transfer, PayPal friends-and-family, or crypto removes every protection you have. On Fiverr, it also violates the terms — and it is the clearest sign the seller knows their service will not survive a dispute.

7. Cold reading sold as insight

Statements that apply to nearly everyone — "you have been hurt by someone you trusted", "you don't show your real self to everyone" — delivered as though they were specific. Test it: if a sentence would fit your neighbour too, it is not information.

8. No scope, no refund position

A listing with no stated format, length, or delivery time is not a service, it is a placeholder. Vague scope makes a dispute unwinnable, because there is nothing to hold the seller to.

9. Requests for personal data

A reading needs your question, and for astrology your date, time and place of birth. It does not need your address, bank details, social security number, or photos of your ID.

The flip side: signs of a decent reader

  • Written scope: questions, format, length, delivery time.
  • Answers a pre-order question specifically and quickly.
  • Says "no" to something — death, medical, legal, or guaranteed outcomes.
  • Reviews mention specifics rather than adjectives.
  • Has one clear price and is not building a ladder.

If you have already paid

  1. Stop the upsell. Do not buy the clearing, the candle, the extra session. The script escalates as long as it is working.
  2. Use the platform. On Fiverr, open a case in the resolution centre with the gig description quoted — this is why scope matters.
  3. Dispute off-platform payments with your bank or card issuer if you were pushed outside the marketplace.
  4. Report the listing. It protects the next person, and marketplaces act on pattern reports.
  5. Talk to someone. Fear-based readings land hard. A friend, or a licensed counsellor if it is affecting you, is worth more than another paid session.

Are all psychic readings scams?

No. Plenty of readers are sincere and many clients find real value in the reflection. The patterns above describe a specific commercial script — manufactured problems and paid solutions — not the whole field.

What if a reader tells me something frightening?

Slow down. It is the most reliable moment to check: is there a paid remedy attached to the fear? If yes, walk away. If you are distressed by it, talk to a friend or a counsellor rather than buying another session.

Is "block removal" ever legitimate?

Some traditions genuinely include clearing work as part of their practice. The test is commercial, not theological: was it mentioned before you paid, is it priced transparently, and is it optional? An upsell discovered mid-reading fails all three.

Can I get my money back on Fiverr?

The resolution centre can cancel an order that was never delivered or did not match its stated scope. It will not refund a reading that simply did not resonate — so read the description and keep your messages on-platform.

How do I report a seller?

Every Fiverr gig page has a report option, and the resolution centre handles disputes with the order record behind you. Keep all communication inside the platform so there is a record.

Why the scripts work: the mechanics of a cold reading

The nine patterns are effective for the same reason, and understanding the mechanism makes all of them easier to spot. A cold reading works by making statements that feel specific but apply to almost everyone, then letting you supply the specificity.

“You have been carrying a disappointment about someone you trusted” is not a fact about you; it is a fact about being an adult. What makes it feel accurate is that you immediately know which disappointment — and you credit the reader for the part you did yourself. The reader then watches your reaction and narrows, which is the moment a guess becomes a reading.

The defence is boring but effective: notice when a statement feels accurate because you filled it in. That is the whole trick, and it is much less impressive once you have seen it from the outside.

The upsell ladder, in full

The single most expensive pattern deserves its own section, because it unfolds over weeks rather than minutes.

It starts with a cheap or free reading that establishes the reader is “picking something up”. Then comes the finding: a block, a curse, an attachment, a difficult aspect. Then the remedy, priced well above the original reading. Then, once that is paid for, a further complication that needs a further session — and the complication is described in terms that make stopping feel risky.

Two things to hold on to. First, the finding always appears after you have paid and engaged, never before. Second, the remedy is always the same shape: another purchase. A genuine concern raised by a reader would come with no price attached.

Method & sources

The nine patterns are the ones most frequently reported by consumers of online spiritual services and most often cited in consumer-protection guidance on psychic fraud: manufactured curses and blocks, fear-based predictions, guaranteed outcomes, urgency, upsell ladders, off-platform payment, cold reading, undefined scope and unnecessary personal data.

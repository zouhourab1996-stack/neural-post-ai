A useful new moon intention-setting check-in does not need a kit or a complicated set of rules. Set aside fifteen minutes, choose one area of life, write up to three intentions that describe actions within your control, and decide on one small step you can take in the next two days.

The astronomical new moon occurs when the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, with its illuminated side facing away from us. It is not visible in the night sky. Some people use this point in the lunar cycle as a symbol of a fresh start. There is no evidence that the phase itself makes a wish come true; the practical value is in pausing, choosing a direction and making a plan you can follow.

This guide walks through choosing a theme, writing clear intentions and reviewing your progress over the next month. Treat the exercise as a reflective practice, not a prediction or professional advice. You do not need to write at the exact astronomical instant. If you want to check the phase time, use the U.S. Naval Observatory's Moon Phases service and convert from Universal Time to your local time zone.

1. What an intention actually is

Intention versus goal

A goal is a result you can measure, like saving a set amount. An intention is the direction and the kind of effort you want to bring, like "I am building a steady savings habit." Goals can fail. An intention is something you can act on today.

Why the new moon is used

The lunar cycle repeats about every 29.5 days. That gives you twelve or thirteen natural check-ins a year, which is more forgiving than a single January resolution. If you miss one, the next is under a month away.

An intention is a direction you can act on today, not a promise you can fail.

2. Before you write: gather your material

Clear a little space

Set aside fifteen minutes with no phone. Sit somewhere you will not be interrupted. A candle is optional.

Review the last cycle

Spend three minutes on these questions before writing anything new:

  • What did I finish since the last new moon?
  • What drained me and is still on my plate?
  • What did I keep putting off?

Pick one area

Choose one theme for the month, such as work, health, money, relationships, or rest. Focus makes the page useful.

One theme per month is plenty. Narrow beats broad.

3. How to word an intention

Use these rules

Good wording is plain and specific.

  • Write in the present tense: "I am" rather than "I will".
  • Describe what you want, not what you want to avoid.
  • Tie it to something you control, such as your habits or your choices.
  • Keep each one to a single sentence.

Examples

VagueClearer intention
I want more moneyI am tracking my spending every Sunday
Be healthierI am walking for twenty minutes after lunch
No more stressI am leaving work at a set time
Find loveI am open to meeting people and saying yes to invitations

If you cannot picture doing it this week, reword it until you can.

4. A step-by-step routine

The fifteen-minute version

Follow this order, because releasing first leaves room for the new.

  1. Breathe slowly five times, with the exhale as long as the inhale.
  2. Write one or two lines about what you are ready to release.
  3. Write up to three intentions using the rules above.
  4. Circle the one that matters most.
  5. Read it aloud once, slowly.
  6. Name one small action you will take in the next 48 hours.

What to do with the page

Keep it somewhere visible: a notebook, a mirror, a desk drawer you open daily. Some people photograph it. Others burn the release note. Either choice is fine, and neither changes the outcome.

The small action within 48 hours is what turns a wish into a plan.

5. Follow up through the month

The full moon check-in

About two weeks later, reread the page. Ask what moved, what stalled, and what you would reword. Our full moon calendar for 2027 helps you plan these checkpoints ahead.

The next new moon

When the next cycle starts, review the old page before writing the new one. Carry forward what is still true and drop what is not. After a few months you will see your own patterns, which is the real value.

Review beats repetition: look back before you look ahead.

6. Common mistakes to avoid

Writing too many intentions

Ten intentions dilute each other. Stay at three or fewer.

Treating it as a guarantee

The ritual is reflective. It supports your choices but does not replace effort, planning, or professional advice on health, money, or legal matters.

Waiting for the perfect minute

The exact minute of the new moon is a nice detail, not a requirement. Within a day or two is fine. If you want more ideas for the evening, try our simple new moon rituals for beginners, or read about the new moon in Scorpio for an example of sign-based themes.

Consistency and honesty matter more than timing and props.

7. Themes by area of life

Work and money

Examples: "I am sending one proposal a week" or "I am reviewing my budget on Sundays."

Health and rest

Examples: "I am going to bed at the same time each night" or "I am drinking water before coffee."

Relationships

Examples: "I am calling a friend every week" or "I am saying what I need clearly and kindly."

Creativity and learning

Examples: "I am practising for ten minutes a day" or "I am finishing one chapter a week."

Pick the area where a small change would help most.

8. A sample month, week by week

Week 1: start

Write your intentions on the new moon night. Take your first small action within 48 hours. Tell one trusted person what you chose, because saying it to someone makes it real.

Week 2: adjust

Check in briefly. Is the action realistic? If you missed it twice, shrink it. Ten minutes done beats an hour planned.

Week 3: review at the full moon

Reread the page. Note one thing that worked and one thing that did not. Cross out any line that no longer fits. You are allowed to change your mind.

Week 4: close the cycle

Write three sentences about what you learned. Decide what carries over to the next new moon and what you are done with. This closing note makes the next page easier to write.

9. Tools that make it easier

Paper or digital

A dedicated notebook lets you flip back through past cycles. A notes app is searchable and always with you. Pick the one you will really open.

A simple tracker

Draw a row of small boxes, one per day, under your top intention. Tick a box when you take the action. Seeing a chain of ticks is quietly motivating, and gaps show you where the plan needs work.

An accountability partner

Share your intentions with a friend and swap check-ins at the full moon. Two people reviewing each other's pages keep each other honest without pressure.

Keep it light

If a month goes badly, do not treat it as failure. Note what got in the way, such as travel, illness, or a heavy work period, and write an easier intention next time. The practice is meant to support you, not grade you.

10. FAQ

Can I set intentions after the exact new moon?

Yes. Most people treat the day of the new moon and the day or two after as the window. Intentions are about starting a cycle, so a short delay changes nothing.

How many intentions should I set?

One to three. If you list more, choose the one you would regret not acting on.

Do I have to say them out loud?

No. Reading aloud helps some people commit, but writing alone works.

Should I write them by hand?

Handwriting slows you down, which tends to improve the wording. A notes app is fine if that is what you will actually use.

Is this the same as manifesting?

It is related but more grounded. You are choosing a direction and a first action, not asking the universe to deliver an outcome.

Start with one sentence

You do not need a perfect ritual. Review the last month, pick one theme, write one clear sentence, and decide on one small action. Repeat next cycle and adjust.

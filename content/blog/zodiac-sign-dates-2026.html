Most zodiac date lists are approximations copied from one another, which is why they disagree. The Sun enters each sign at a specific moment, and that moment moves by roughly six hours a year — so in any given year a couple of the usual start dates are a day off. Below are the 2026 ingress times in UTC, next to the conventional date ranges you will see everywhere else.

Why the dates move

The tropical zodiac is defined by the seasons, not by the constellations: 0° Aries is the moment of the March equinox, and every other sign boundary is measured from there. But the calendar year (365.2422 days) and the leap-year cycle do not divide evenly into twelve, so the equinox drifts against the clock. Leap years pull it back, and in 2027 the Aries ingress falls at 20:25 UTC on 20 March — about six hours later than 2026.

Over a few years this pushes several boundaries across midnight, which is exactly why printed date ranges differ between websites and why two sites can both be "right" for different years.

Born on a cusp: how to settle it

A cusp birth is any birth within about 24 hours of an ingress. There is no blended sign — the Sun is in one sign or the other, and only your exact time and place of birth determine which. Three steps:

  1. Get your exact birth time from a birth certificate, hospital record or family record. "Morning" is not enough; an ingress at 09:38 UTC can land on either side of a local midnight.
  2. Enter the time and place into any ephemeris-based chart calculator. The calculation is deterministic — every competent tool returns the same Sun sign.
  3. Check the ascendant too. If you have a birth time, you also have a rising sign, and it often explains why your Sun sign never quite felt like the whole story.

If you genuinely cannot recover a birth time, most astrologers work with a solar chart (Sun placed at the ascendant) rather than guessing — and you should be sceptical of anyone who charges extra to "rectify" a time they cannot derive.

What each element and modality means

Beyond the dates, the twelve signs sort into two useful grids:

  • Elements — Fire (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius): action and enthusiasm. Earth (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn): practicality and material results. Air (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius): ideas and exchange. Water (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces): feeling and attachment.
  • Modalities — Cardinal (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn): starts things. Fixed (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius): sustains things. Mutable (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces): adapts things.

Element and modality together explain most of what people mean by "signs just click or clash": two Fire signs share a tempo, and two Fixed signs both dig in.

Why do different websites list different zodiac dates?

Because the Sun's ingress time shifts about six hours a year, and printed ranges get copied without being recalculated. Most lists are correct for most years; the disagreement appears only in the signs whose boundary falls near midnight.

Am I two signs if I was born on a cusp?

No. There is no blended Sun sign — the Sun is in one sign or the other at the moment of your birth. What cusp births often produce is a mix of traits from the ascendant, the Moon or other personal planets, which is why the blend feels real.

Is my rising sign more accurate than my Sun sign?

Neither is more accurate; they answer different questions. The Sun describes your core drive and the ascendant describes your outward style and how you handle new situations. Many people find horoscopes written for the rising sign fit better.

Do zodiac dates change because of the precession of the equinoxes?

In the tropical zodiac used by Western astrology, no: the signs stay fixed to the seasons, so 0° Aries is always the March equinox. Precession matters for sidereal astrology, which anchors signs to the constellations and therefore gives different dates.

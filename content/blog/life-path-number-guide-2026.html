Your life path number comes from your date of birth, and the method is fixed: reduce the date to a single digit — with three exceptions. It takes about thirty seconds, and knowing your own number before you buy a reading is the simplest way to tell a careful numerologist from a generated report.

The method, step by step

Take your full date of birth and reduce each part — month, day, year — to a single digit, then add those together and reduce again.

Worked example: 14 July 1995
Month: 7 → 7
Day: 14 → 1 + 4 = 5
Year: 1995 → 1 + 9 + 9 + 5 = 24 → 2 + 4 = 6
Total: 7 + 5 + 6 = 18 → 1 + 8 = 9
Life path = 9

The one thing to watch: if your total lands on 11, 22, or 33 before the final reduction, most numerologists stop there — see the master numbers section below.

Master numbers: 11, 22, 33

These are the exception to the reduce-to-one-digit rule. If your month, day and year add to 11, 22 or 33, most practitioners keep the double digit, treating it as an intensified form of its single-digit root:

  • 11 → an intensified 2 — the "master" reading is intuition and nervous energy held together.
  • 22 → an intensified 4 — the capacity to build at scale, with the pressure that comes with it.
  • 33 → an intensified 6 — the caregiver at its most demanding.

If your total is 11 and your reader reports a 2 without mentioning 11 at all, that is worth a question. Both conventions exist; the point is that they should have one and say which.

Check your own first

Why this matters commercially: the calculation is deterministic. If a paid reading gives you a different life path number than your own arithmetic, ask why before reading another word — either they used a different convention, or the report was generated without anyone checking.

The other core numbers

Life path is the headline, but most readings cover four or five:

  • Expression / destiny — from your full birth name; what your talents point toward.
  • Soul urge — from the vowels; what you want underneath the habits.
  • Personality — from the consonants; how you come across before people know you.
  • Personal year — birth date plus the current year, reduced; the flavour of the year you are in, cycling 1 to 9.

Name-based numbers are the ones most open to disagreement, because the Chaldean and Pythagorean systems assign letters differently. If a reading's name numbers surprise you, ask which system they used before assuming an error.

Do I reduce 11 to 2?

Most practitioners keep 11, 22 and 33 un-reduced as master numbers. Some systems reduce them. Either is defensible — what matters is that a reader is consistent and tells you which convention they use.

Which is correct: reducing the whole date at once, or each part first?

Reducing month, day and year separately first is the most widely taught method and the one most readers use. Reducing the entire string of digits at once usually reaches the same result, but not always — which is why the step-by-step method is the safer default.

Does my life path number change?

No. It is derived from your date of birth and stays fixed for life. What changes is your personal year, which cycles 1 through 9 as the years pass.

Do I use my birth name or my married name?

Only the life path uses your birth date. Name-based numbers usually start from the name recorded at birth, with later changes treated as a layer — ask the reader which they use.

Is any of this evidence-based?

No. Numerology is a symbolic system with no scientific support. Many people find it a useful prompt for thinking about their own patterns, which is a reasonable reason to use it — and a reason not to make significant decisions on it alone.

Worked examples

Reducing a date of birth to a single digit is simple, but two rules catch people out, so here they are in full.

Example one, no master number. 14 July 1991. Month: 0+7 = 7. Day: 1+4 = 5. Year: 1+9+9+1 = 20, then 2+0 = 2. Total: 7 + 5 + 2 = 14, then 1+4 = 5.

Example two, a master number appears. 11 November 1984. Month: 1+1 = 2. Day: 11 — leave it as 11, do not reduce it to 2. Year: 1+9+8+4 = 22, which is also a master number and is left as 22. Total: 2 + 11 + 22 = 35, then 3+5 = 8. Note the master numbers were preserved through the addition and only the final total was reduced — that is the convention.

The two rules: reduce each of the month, day and year separately first, and never reduce 11, 22 or 33 when they appear as a component.

Why two calculators give you two different numbers

This is the most common source of confusion and it has a mundane explanation: the systems disagree. The Pythagorean system, which maps letters and numbers 1 to 9 in sequence, is the Western default and what most websites use. The Chaldean system assigns different values and has no zero, so it frequently lands on a different number for the same date.

Neither is a mistake. If a calculator tells you 6 and a reader tells you 8, ask which system they used before concluding that one of you is wrong — and then be consistent, because mixing systems mid-reading produces nonsense.

Master numbers: the honest version

Master numbers are 11, 22 and 33, and the rule across most schools is that they are not reduced when they appear as a component of the calculation. In practice, what this means is that someone whose working total is 11 is described as having the qualities of the 2 with an added intensity, not as a different person entirely.

Be sceptical of readings that treat a master number as a rank. It is a convention about how to handle a double digit, and the elaborate significance attached to it varies enormously between practitioners — which is itself a hint that it is interpretation, not arithmetic.

What to actually do with the number

The number is a prompt, not a verdict. The way most people find it useful is as a structured excuse to write down what is already true: if your reading describes a tension between independence and responsibility, the useful part is deciding what to do about that tension, not the fact that a calculation produced it.

Two things it should never be: a reason to turn down a job, a relationship or a move, and a basis for a financial decision. If a reading is pushing you towards a specific material action, the push is coming from the reader, not from the number.

Method & sources

The calculation method described is the standard Pythagorean approach used by most English-language numerologists.

What a Scorpio new moon actually is

A new moon is the dark moon: the Sun and Moon conjunct, the lunar face unlit, the sky at its quietest. Astrologically it's the planting phase of the cycle — intentions set in the dark, before there's anything to show for them.

Scorpio is fixed water, traditionally ruled by Mars and modernly by Pluto. Its territory is the eighth house: shared money (debt, investments, what's mine-versus-ours), intimacy and trust, the things people don't say out loud, and endings — real ones, the kind you don't undo. Where Taurus (its opposite) keeps and maintains, Scorpio cuts and regenerates. A Scorpio new moon is the year's deepest planting hole: whatever you seed here grows roots before it grows leaves.

Which is why the intensity-word articles all fail the same way: they tell you to transform and forget to tell you that Scorpio transformation is a trade. Something is born; something dies. You don't get the rebirth without naming the ending. Keep that pairing in mind — it's the design of the ritual at the bottom of this page.

What to plant — and what to end

Scorpio's eighth-house territory makes some intentions native to this moon and others foreign. Native:

  • Money that involves another party — paying off debt, restructuring a loan, negotiating a raise, launching something you've already built in the dark.
  • Trust and intimacy — the conversation you've been circling for months, the boundary that would change a relationship, the decision to stop performing closeness you don't feel.
  • A private discipline — the habit no one would ever see you doing, which is precisely why Scorpio respects it.

Foreign (save these for the Sagittarius new moon a year from now, or the Gemini full moon on November 24):

  • Anything whose main feature is being seen doing it — the Mars–Jupiter square will eat it.
  • Brand launches, big announcements and signing ceremonies before November 13 — Mercury retrograde in the planting sign makes paperwork the weak link. Ship after the unlock.
  • Revenge, in any packaging. Scorpio's shadow loves this moon; the South Node in Leo says the story you'd tell about it is the actual bait.

The 20-minute dark ritual (no purchases)

Scorpio rituals should be private, quiet and cost nothing — the sign can smell a performance and a price tag from orbit. Twenty minutes, ideally within 24–48 hours of the new moon (the evening of November 9 is perfect):

  1. Go dark (3 minutes). One candle, screens off, door closed. Scorpio's moon is the most private of the year — the first act is removing the audience.
  2. The ending (7 minutes). Write down the one thing you're ending: the debt pattern, the dynamic, the version of you that maintained it. Not a paragraph — a sentence. Then destroy the paper safely (tear it, or burn it over a plate). Scorpio is the one sign of the year where this gesture is not theater; it's the actual mechanics.
  3. The seed (5 minutes). One sentence, present tense, private: what begins because that ended. Write it somewhere durable — you will need it on November 11 and again on the 13th.
  4. The date (2 minutes). Open your calendar and place one 30-minute block on November 13: "First step." When both planets station direct that day, the sky will be daring you to start. Have the step already chosen so the answer is yes.
  5. Silence (3 minutes). Sit with the candle. No gratitude list, no vision board — Scorpio's moon prefers the honest dark to the mood board. Let it be uncomfortable; that's the sign you did it right.

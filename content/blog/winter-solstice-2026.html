The winter solstice is the turning point. After it, the days in the Northern Hemisphere start getting longer, slowly at first, then faster through January. In 2026 it falls on a Monday afternoon for North America and a Monday evening for the UK. It also sits in an unusually busy sky week: Jupiter has just turned retrograde, and the year's closest full moon follows two days later.

Here's the exact time in your zone, how much daylight you really get, why your darkest evening isn't the solstice, and what astrologers make of the Sun's move into Capricorn.

The solstice is a single instant, the same moment everywhere on Earth. Only the clock reading changes. That's why Australians mark it on December 22, and why some calendars print December 22 for the Southern Hemisphere. For the US, Canada and the UK in 2026, the date is December 21.

And how fast does it come back? Not fast at first. By December 31, New York gains only about 3 minutes of daylight over the solstice. London gains about 5 and Anchorage about 10. The gains accelerate through January and peak near the March equinox, which falls on March 20, 2027, at 20:24 UTC.

The Earliest Sunset and Latest Sunrise Puzzle

Many people expect the solstice to bring both the earliest sunset and the latest sunrise. It doesn't. Because Earth's orbit is an ellipse and its axis is tilted, solar noon drifts later through December (this is the "equation of time"). The result: sunsets start getting later before the solstice, while sunrises keep getting later until after it.

So if your evenings already feel a little lighter by the solstice, you're right. The mornings are the part that keeps dragging. And the closer you live to the equator, the further apart these dates spread.

What Actually Happens at the Solstice

At 20:50 UTC on December 21, the Sun reaches its farthest point south in the sky, a declination of about −23.44°. At that moment it stands directly over the Tropic of Capricorn, which is literally where the tropic got its name. After that it starts moving north again. The word comes from the Latin sol (Sun) and sistere (to stand still), because the Sun's rising point on the horizon seems to pause before reversing.

Two misconceptions are worth clearing up. First, winter isn't caused by distance. Earth is actually close to the Sun in December, and perihelion, our closest point, comes on about January 3, 2027. The cold comes from the tilt: low Sun, short days. Second, the solstice is the astronomical start of winter. Meteorologists start winter on December 1 so seasonal statistics line up with whole months.

In the tropical zodiac used by Western astrology, the signs are tied to the seasons on purpose. The December solstice is, by definition, 0° Capricorn. So the solstice moment is the Sun's ingress into Capricorn, and Capricorn season 2026 runs from December 21, 20:50 UTC, to January 20, 2027, 07:27 UTC (2:27 a.m. Eastern), when the Sun enters Aquarius. Winter Solstice Traditions Around the World

People have marked midwinter for thousands of years, mostly with light, food and staying up late. A few traditions worth knowing:

  • Stonehenge, England. English Heritage opens the stone circle for free "managed access" at dawn on Tuesday, December 22, 2026. The car park opens at 5:15 a.m., sunrise is about 8:09 a.m., and parking must be pre-booked. The monument's main axis lines up with the midsummer sunrise and the midwinter sunset.
  • Newgrange, Ireland. At this 5,000-year-old passage tomb in County Meath, built around 3200 BC, sunlight enters a "roof-box" on the mornings around the solstice. It travels about 19 metres (62 ft) along the passage and lights the inner chamber for around 17 minutes. Chamber access is by lottery, and the OPW livestreams the sunrise.
  • Yule. The Germanic and Norse midwinter festival gave English the Yule log, evergreen decorations and, via the old name for the December full moon, the "Moon Before Yule". Modern pagans celebrate Yule at the solstice.
  • Dongzhi, China and East Asia. The "arrival of winter" festival is a family feast, often with tangyuan (sweet rice balls) or dumplings, celebrating the return of longer days.
  • Shab-e Yalda, Iran. On the longest night, families stay up together eating pomegranates and watermelon and reading poetry, especially Hafez.
  • Soyal, Hopi people of Arizona. A ceremony that welcomes the Sun's return and marks the start of a new ceremonial year.
  • Saturnalia, ancient Rome. A December festival of feasting, gifts and role reversals, held in honour of Saturn, the same planet that rules Capricorn in astrology.

Winter Solstice Rituals for 2026

You don't need to travel to a stone circle. These five simple practices fit a Monday in December:

  1. Watch the sunrise on December 22. The solstice itself happens in the afternoon or evening for most readers, so the first sunrise after it, on Tuesday morning, is the traditional moment. Ten quiet minutes facing southeast is enough.
  2. Light in the dark. Turn off every light at sunset on the 21st, sit in the dark for a few minutes, then light one candle or lamp. It's the oldest solstice ritual there is, and kids love it.
  3. The longest-night list. Write down what 2026 taught you, one line per month. Capricorn rewards looking at the record before setting the goal.
  4. One small Capricorn goal. Pick a single goal for 2027 that is boring, specific and measurable. "Walk 20 minutes after lunch" beats "get healthy". Revisit it at the Cold Moon on December 23 and again at the Aquarius ingress on January 20.
  5. Feed someone. Every midwinter tradition on the list above involves a shared meal. Invite someone who might be alone this week.

FAQ

What time is the winter solstice in 2026?

Monday, December 21, 2026, at 20:50 UTC. That's 3:50 p.m. Eastern, 2:50 p.m. Central, 12:50 p.m. Pacific and 8:50 p.m. GMT in the UK. In eastern Australia it's 7:50 a.m. on Tuesday, December 22.

Is December 21 the shortest day of 2026?

Yes, for the Northern Hemisphere. In New York, December 21 has 9 hours 15 minutes of daylight, about 5 hours 50 minutes less than the June solstice. In London it's 7 hours 50 minutes.

When do the days start getting longer after the 2026 solstice?

Immediately, but slowly. New York gains only about 3 minutes of daylight by December 31 and London about 5. Evenings have actually been getting lighter since the earliest sunset in early to mid December.

When does Capricorn season start and end in 2026?

Capricorn season begins at the solstice, December 21, 2026, at 20:50 UTC, and ends when the Sun enters Aquarius on January 20, 2027, at 07:27 UTC (2:27 a.m. Eastern).

What is the spiritual meaning of the winter solstice?

Across many cultures it symbolises the return of the light: the darkest point of the year, after which the days grow longer. In astrology it marks the Sun's entry into Capricorn, a time associated with planning, commitment and steady effort. These are symbolic readings, not predictions.

When is Stonehenge open for the winter solstice 2026?

English Heritage is offering free managed access at sunrise on Tuesday, December 22, 2026. The car park opens at 5:15 a.m., sunrise is about 8:09 a.m., and parking must be pre-booked.

Why isn't the earliest sunset on the solstice?

Because solar noon shifts later through December, a result of Earth's elliptical orbit and tilted axis. Sunsets start getting later about two weeks before the solstice, while sunrises keep getting later until early January.

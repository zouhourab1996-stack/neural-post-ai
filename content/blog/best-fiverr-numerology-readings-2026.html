Vetting checks that actually work

  • They ask for your full legal birth name — not just your first name. Expression and soul urge numbers are calculated from the whole name as recorded at birth.
  • They state the method. Pythagorean (the common western system) vs Chaldean produce different numbers for the same name. Neither is "wrong", but a reader should say which they use.
  • They mention mastery numbers without being asked. If your calculation lands on 11, 22, or 33, a competent reader will address it directly.
  • Reviews describe specifics — "she explained why my expression number clashes with my job" beats generic praise.
  • The report is not just adjectives. Three pages of "you are creative, intuitive and sometimes impatient" is a template.
Name-change question. Most practitioners use the name on your birth record as the baseline and treat a changed or married name as a layer on top. If a seller insists your numbers "changed completely" and offers a paid recalculation, that is an upsell, not a system.

What are master numbers and do I have one?

11, 22 and 33 are traditionally left un-reduced because they are treated as a concentrated form of 2, 4 and 6. If your date-of-birth reduction lands on one of them before the final step, most readers keep it as a master number rather than reducing it.

Do I use my birth name or my current name?

Most systems use the full name as recorded at birth as the baseline, then treat later name changes as an additional layer. Ask the reader which convention they use before you order.

Is numerology compatible with astrology?

They are separate systems with separate methods, and plenty of readers work in both. There is no conflict — but a reader should be clear which system any given statement comes from.

Can numerology pick a wedding date or a business name?

This is one of the more common paid add-ons. Treat it as a preference filter, not a decision engine: if a name is legally available, memorable and you like it, that counts for far more than its number.

How long does a numerology reading take to deliver?

Written reports usually arrive in 1–3 days; recordings in 24–48 hours. Same-day delivery is common at the lower end and usually indicates a generated report.

Three calculation methods, three different answers

The most important thing to know before you pay: there is no single agreed number. Several calculation systems are in use, and for the same person they produce different results.

SystemHow it assigns valuesWhere it is used
PythagoreanLetters mapped 1–9 in sequenceThe Western default; assumed by most websites
ChaldeanDifferent letter values and no zeroCommon in Indian practice; often yields different numbers
KabbalahMystical letter assignmentsLess common; sometimes added as a third layer

This explains the most frequent complaint: someone calculates a 6 on one site, orders a reading, and is told they are an 8. Neither is necessarily wrong — they are different systems. A good reader states their method in the report. One who does not makes verification impossible.

Which name do you use — and what about marriage?

The full birth name is the basis in most schools, on the reasoning that the original name carries the pattern. Practice varies, though: some readers use the name you go by today, and some calculate both and show you the difference.

Ask before ordering, because it changes the result entirely when a name has changed. If your report will touch a name altered by marriage or anything else, say so explicitly in your order — a reader calculating from the wrong name will produce a perfectly crafted report about someone else.

What a numerology report should never claim

The limits deserve stating plainly. Numerology should not be used to predict a medical diagnosis, the outcome of a legal case, a share price, or a date of death. The system works by symbolic assignment of numbers, and it contains no mechanism that generates any of those things.

The most practically dangerous claim is timing. Statements like “your number peaks this month” read as information but are unverifiable in principle. A reader attaching specific timing to financial or medical decisions has gone beyond what any system supports — and that is the point at which to stop.

Compatibility numbers: what is actually being compared

Numerology compatibility readings usually compare each person's life path number and then describe how the two numbers interact. That calculation is consistent and verifiable — you can check it yourself for free — which makes it the easiest quality test on a paid report.

What it does not do is tell you about a person. Any framework that reduces a relationship to two numbers is describing a pattern, not an outcome. A reading that admits that limit is more useful than one claiming to settle the question of a relationship.

The “personal year” and other timing claims

The personal year is calculated from your life path number plus the current year, reduced to a single digit said to characterise that year. The arithmetic is simple and transparent, which is precisely why it is popular.

The problem is not the calculation but the use. The advice that follows the result — “a 9 year, finish what is unresolved” — is general life advice wearing the costume of a computation. Enjoy it if it helps you structure an annual review; do not make a major financial or career decision on the strength of it.

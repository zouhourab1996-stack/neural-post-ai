The first major eclipse season of 2027 begins with a striking event: an annular solar eclipse on February 6, paired with a New Moon in Aquarius. The phrase “ring of fire” makes it sound as though the whole world will see a glowing circle around the Sun. It will not. The complete ring is visible only from a relatively narrow corridor, while a much wider region sees a partial eclipse — and many places see nothing at all.

This guide separates the astronomical event from its astrological symbolism. Where can you see the ring of fire?

An annular eclipse occurs when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun while appearing slightly too small in the sky to cover the Sun completely. The result is a bright ring around the Moon’s silhouette. The path of annularity for February 6 crosses parts of South America, the Atlantic Ocean and West Africa.

Countries along the annular path

  • South America: Chile, Argentina, Uruguay and Brazil.
  • West Africa: Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and Nigeria.

Not every location in those countries lies inside the narrow ring path. A city may see only a partial eclipse even when another part of the same country sees annularity. Check the exact map and local circumstances for your chosen town.

Where is a partial eclipse visible?

A much broader region may see at least part of the Moon crossing the Sun. NASA lists parts of South America and Africa, as well as areas of the South Atlantic, South Pacific and Antarctica. The exact amount of the Sun covered changes substantially from one location to another.

For example, the February 6 event is not the major Tunisian eclipse. Tunis and much of North Africa are outside the annular path, and location-specific sources do not list Tunis as a viewing location for this event. The highly anticipated 2027 eclipse for Tunisia is the total solar eclipse of August 2, 2027, which will be seen as a deep partial eclipse from Tunis and as totality only along a narrower path elsewhere in the country.

How to watch a solar eclipse safely

Never look directly at the Sun without proper solar protection. This remains true during an annular eclipse and during every partial phase. The ring of sunlight is still bright enough to cause serious and permanent eye injury.
  • Use eclipse glasses that comply with the ISO 12312-2 standard and inspect them before use.
  • Put the viewers on before looking toward the Sun and look away before removing them.
  • Do not use ordinary sunglasses, smoked glass, CDs, photographic film or improvised filters.
  • Never look through binoculars, a telescope or a camera optical viewfinder unless the device has a suitable solar filter securely fitted to the front of the optics.
  • For an indirect view, use a pinhole projector and look only at the projected image — never through the pinhole toward the Sun.
  • Supervise children and follow the instructions supplied with certified viewers.

Unlike a total solar eclipse, an annular eclipse has no brief phase when the Sun is completely covered. There is no safe naked-eye interval.

What does the Aquarius solar eclipse mean in astrology?

In tropical astrology, the New Moon and eclipse occur at approximately 17°38′ Aquarius. Aquarius is traditionally associated with communities, friendships, networks, collective ideals, technology and the tension between belonging and independence. Astrologers often interpret a solar eclipse as a more disruptive or consequential symbolic beginning than an ordinary New Moon.

That is an astrological interpretation, not an astronomical effect or a scientifically established forecast. An eclipse does not determine personal outcomes. A useful way to work with the symbolism is to treat it as a prompt to review the groups, systems and long-term plans you choose to participate in.

The eclipse does not happen alone

Solar and lunar eclipses tend to arrive in an eclipse season. About two weeks after the February 6 annular solar eclipse, a penumbral lunar eclipse occurs on February 20–21, 2027, with the Full Moon in Virgo. That second event has its own visibility map and local timings; it should not be confused with the February 6 solar eclipse.

Frequently asked questions

When is the annular solar eclipse in February 2027?

It occurs on Saturday, February 6, 2027. The New Moon is exact at 15:56 UTC, while greatest eclipse occurs at approximately 15:59:37 UTC.

What time is the February 2027 solar eclipse in New York?

The New Moon is at 10:56 a.m. EST. The global greatest-eclipse instant is about 10:59:37 a.m. EST, but that is not the local maximum for New York, which is outside the path of visibility.

Which countries see the ring of fire?

Parts of Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Brazil, Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and Nigeria lie along the annular path. Not every town in those countries sees the ring.

Can I see the February 6, 2027 eclipse from Tunisia?

Tunis is not listed as a viewing location for the February 6 annular eclipse. Tunisia's major 2027 solar eclipse is on August 2, when a deep partial eclipse is visible from Tunis.

Is an annular eclipse safe to watch without eclipse glasses?

No. The Sun remains visible as a bright ring throughout annularity. Use certified ISO 12312-2 eclipse viewers or a properly filtered indirect viewing method.

Is the Aquarius solar eclipse a scientific prediction?

No. The eclipse date, timing and visibility are astronomical calculations. The Aquarius themes and sign-by-sign meanings are symbolic astrological interpretations, not evidence-based predictions.

Key Takeaways

  • Two peak nights in 2026: the South Taurids around November 4–5 and the North Taurids around November 11–12.
  • Expect about 5 meteors an hour from each branch under dark skies, and roughly 10 when they overlap. This is a slow shower, not a busy one.
  • The fireballs are the reason to watch. About 1% of Taurids are fireballs in a normal year. 2026 is not a swarm year.
  • The Moon helps. A thin waning crescent on November 4–5 and a new Moon on November 9 keep the sky dark.
  • Best time: around midnight to 3 a.m., when the constellation Taurus is high.

The Taurids will not give you a meteor every minute. On a good night you may count a handful in an hour. But they move slowly, they glow for a long moment, and now and then one lights up the ground like a flare. That is why people call the brightest ones Halloween fireballs.

If you have read other guides, you may have noticed that every site gives a different peak date. This guide explains why, gives you the dates that matter in 2026, and shows how to plan around the Moon.

Why Websites Disagree on the Dates

Search for "Taurid meteor shower 2026" and you will find peaks on October 10, October 12–13, October 14, November 4–5 and November 12. They are not all wrong. They answer slightly different questions.

SourceDate it givesWhat it means
American Meteor SocietyOct 14 and Nov 5 (South); Nov 11–12 (North)Two parts of the South Taurids: the annual component and the fireball-swarm component
EarthSky (RASC handbook)Nov 5 at 19 UTC; Nov 12 at 18 UTCPredicted single maxima for each branch

Royal Museums GreenwichOct 10 (South); Nov 12 (North)An earlier South Taurid maximum

EarthSky says it plainly: both branches "don't have very definite peaks" and they "ramble along" through October and November. The two streams overlap from late October into early November, so you can see Taurids on many nights.

The Southern Taurids are a complex shower made up of two main components: the annual component, which peaks on October 14th, and the fireball-swarm component, which peaks on November 5th.American Meteor Society, 2026/2027 calendar

What to do with that: treat November 4–5 and November 11–12 as your two best tries, and enjoy any clear night in between.

Sources quote 18%, 22% or 25% lit for November 4–5. That is a timing effect, since the Moon's lit share changes through the night. The point is the same in every case: a thin crescent that rises late.

A Simple Viewing Plan

  1. Pick your night. November 4–5 for the best chance of a fireball. November 11–12 for the darkest sky.
  2. Go out around 11 p.m. and stay until about 3 a.m. Taurus rises in the early evening and sits highest near midnight.
  3. Find a dark spot. A park, a field or a dark yard works. Face south-east early and south around midnight, about halfway up the sky.
  4. Give your eyes 20 to 30 minutes. Keep your phone dim or in red-light mode.
  5. Look wide. Do not stare at Taurus. Taurid meteors can appear anywhere, and their paths trace back toward the Pleiades.
  6. Skip the binoculars. A wide view of the sky beats a close-up.
Be realistic: with rates near 5 an hour, you may count only a few meteors in an hour, and some hours show none. Bring a blanket and enjoy the night sky. A single bright fireball makes the wait worthwhile.

Predicted Peak Time in Your Time Zone

The RASC handbook, quoted by EarthSky, predicts the South Taurid maximum for 19:00 UTC on November 5 and the North Taurid maximum for 18:00 UTC on November 12. Since the maximum is broad, an hour or two either way changes little. Still, it helps to know when each time falls in your day.

Most of the Americas see the maximum in daylight, so the nights on either side are your best bet. Both showers keep running for weeks, which is why a clear night close to the date is fine.

US and Europe have both moved to standard time by November 5, which is why the offsets above use UTC−5, UTC−8 and UTC+1.

Five Tips for a Better Taurid Night

  1. Check the cloud forecast two days ahead. Clouds beat the Moon every time. If your first night is cloudy, try the next one.
  2. Dress for the cold. Early November nights chill you fast when you sit still. Wear one more layer than you think you need.
  3. Use a reclined chair. You will look up for hours, and a lying position saves your neck.
  4. Keep a simple count. Write down the time and each meteor you see. It turns a slow night into a small science project, and the numbers are fun to compare with the table above.
  5. Try a phone or camera. Use a tripod, a wide lens, a 10 to 20 second exposure and a high ISO. Point at the Pleiades and take a burst of shots for an hour. With luck, one frame catches a fireball.

What a normal Taurid hour looks like

Most hours bring zero to five meteors. They arrive without a pattern, sometimes two in a minute and then nothing for twenty. They cross the sky slowly, taking a second or more, and many leave no trail at all. The bright ones are the exceptions that stay with you.

Simple pack list

  • A reclining chair or foam mat
  • A warm blanket or sleeping bag
  • A hat and gloves
  • A red flashlight or a phone in red-light mode
  • A hot drink and a snack
  • A star-map app to find Taurus and the Pleiades

Halloween Fireballs: What Is Real

A fireball is a meteor brighter than the planet Venus. The Taurids produce more of them than most showers, which is why this shower gets so much attention.

  • In most years about 1% of Taurid meteors are fireballs. Visual observers see around one every 20 hours.
  • In swarm years the share can reach about 7%, and observers may see several in one night.
  • Swarm years so far: 2008, 2015 and 2022. The next one is predicted for 2032.
  • 2026 is a normal year. You have a fair chance at one bright slow meteor if you watch for several hours.

Taurid fireballs travel slowly and often break into pieces, with colors from orange to green. If you see one, report it. The American Meteor Society takes fireball reports online, and each report helps map the path.

Comet Encke, the Source

The dust behind the South Taurids comes from Comet 2P/Encke. It has the shortest orbit of any major comet, about 3.3 years, and at its closest it swings in as near the Sun as Mercury does. The next return is in July 2030.

Many scientists think Encke is a leftover piece of a much larger comet that broke up around 20,000 years ago. The pieces and the dust they shed make up what astronomers call the Encke Complex. The North Taurids likely come from a related stream, and EarthSky notes that no single asteroid may explain them.

Spiritual Meaning, the Honest Version

Meteors have carried meaning for people for a very long time, and the Halloween timing makes the Taurids feel special. Some readers treat them as a moment to let go of something before winter, which fits the Scorpio season theme of release and renewal. That is a personal ritual, and it can be a good one.

There is no scientific link between a meteor shower and events in your life. The Taurids are comet dust burning up in the air. If you want a reflection practice, use the quiet hours outside as a reason to slow down, write a note about what you want to release, and enjoy the sky.

FAQ

When is the Taurid meteor shower in 2026?

The South Taurids are active from about September 20 to November 20 and the North Taurids from October 20 to December 10. The best nights are November 4-5 for the South Taurids and November 11-12 for the North Taurids.

How many Taurid meteors will I see per hour?

Under dark skies, about 5 an hour from each branch, and around 10 an hour when both overlap. The count is low, but the meteors are slow and often bright.

Why do websites give different peak dates for the Taurids?

The Taurids have a broad, flat maximum instead of one sharp peak. The South Taurids also have two parts, an early annual component around October 13-14 and a fireball-rich part around November 4-5, so sources pick different dates.

What are the Halloween fireballs?

Halloween fireballs is a nickname for bright Taurid meteors seen around late October and early November. Most years only about 1% of Taurids are fireballs. In rare swarm years the share can reach 7%.

Is 2026 a Taurid swarm year?

No. Swarm years brought extra fireballs in 2008, 2015 and 2022, and the next one is predicted for 2032. In 2026 expect a few fireballs on a good night, not dozens.

Will the Moon block the Taurids?

Not much. On November 4-5 the Moon is a thin waning crescent that rises in the early morning hours. The new Moon falls on November 9, so the North Taurid night of November 11-12 is almost moonless.

Full Moon in Gemini, November 24, 2026: Uranus Crashes the Party

The November full moon peaks on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, at 14:53 UTC. That is 9:53 a.m. in New York, 6:53 a.m. in Los Angeles and 2:53 p.m. in London. It lands at about 2° of Gemini, directly opposite a Sun that moved into Sagittarius on November 22. Astrologers read a Gemini full moon as a moment when news, conversations and half-formed opinions come into the open. This one has an extra detail most calendars skip: Uranus sits almost on top of the Moon.

Because the Moon enters Gemini less than four hours before it turns full, the whole event sits inside one sign. In the Americas the exact moment falls in the morning, so the Moon looks full on the nights of both November 23 and 24. In Europe, Africa and Asia the night of the 24th is the one to watch.

Why Uranus changes the flavor of this full moon

A full moon is always a Sun–Moon opposition. What shapes its mood in astrology is whatever else touches it. One ephemeris-based guide places the Moon at 2°20' of Gemini and Uranus at roughly 3°44', which puts the two within about a degree and a half. Uranus is the planet astrologers associate with sudden change, disruption and the thing nobody saw coming. Gemini rules information and communication.

Put those together and the traditional reading is easy to state. A message you did not expect, a changed plan, a glitch in a device or an app, a conversation that goes somewhere new. Astrologers would add that the Sagittarius side of the opposition asks you to step back from the details and ask what they add up to. Facts on the Gemini side, meaning on the Sagittarius side.

That is a symbolic reading, not a forecast. We are not aware of reliable evidence that lunar phases or planetary alignments change how people behave, and astrologers themselves usually frame this as a prompt for reflection. If a week feels chaotic around November 24, the calendar is more likely a convenient label than a cause.

Is it really a supermoon?

Several outlets call this one a supermoon, and the claim depends entirely on which definition you accept. A lunar calendar published for the month gives the Moon's distance at the exact moment of fullness as about 360,765 km, with perigee (closest approach) following roughly thirty hours later at about 359,347 km. It describes the November full moon as the second closest of 2026, after the one on December 24. Those figures come from a single source, so treat them as approximate.

The word itself is the interesting part. It was coined in 1979 by the astrologer Richard Nolle in Dell Horoscope magazine, who defined it as a new or full moon at or near, within 90 percent of, its closest approach to Earth. Nolle never explained the 90 percent cutoff, and astronomers do not use the term. Their name for the same thing is a perigee-syzygy.

The definitions also disagree with each other. A strict version counts only the single closest full moon of the year, which allows at most two supermoons per year. A broader version counts any full moon near perigee, which allows several. EarthSky points out that even the 90 percent rule changes depending on whether you measure against the year's closest perigee or against each month's own orbit. So the honest answer for November 24 is that it is a supermoon on the broad lists and might not be on a strict one.

Whatever the label, the visual difference is small. Reference sources describe a supermoon as at most about 14 percent larger and 30 percent brighter than a typical full moon, and most people cannot tell by eye without a comparison.

Why two sites give two exact times

Most sources, including the Farmers' Almanac and lunaf.com, list the peak at 14:53 UTC. At least one moon-calendar site lists 14:58 UTC. A five-minute gap like that usually comes down to the ephemeris or the rounding a site uses, and it does not matter unless you are timing something to the minute. For a ritual or a photo, the practical window is hours, not minutes. If you want to verify, NASA and the US Naval Observatory publish phase times you can compare against your own clock.

Where this sits in the week

The Moon stays in Gemini until Thanksgiving morning in the United States, so a Gemini full moon overlaps with travel, family conversations and plenty of talking. Frequently Asked Questions

What time is the full moon on November 24, 2026?
It peaks at 14:53 UTC: 9:53 a.m. EST, 8:53 a.m. CST, 6:53 a.m. PST and 2:53 p.m. GMT. The moment is the same everywhere. Only the clock reading changes.

Is the November 2026 full moon a supermoon?
By the broad definition, yes, it falls close to perigee. By the strict definition of the single closest full moon of the year, one source says the December 24 full moon is closer. The term has no official astronomical definition.

Why is it called the Beaver Moon?
By tradition, the first full moon of November takes that name. The Farmers' Almanac also lists Frost Moon, Mourning Moon and Trading Moon, and notes that it is the last full moon before the winter solstice.

Which night should I look at the Moon?
In North and South America, the Moon looks full on both November 23 and 24 because the peak falls in the morning. In Europe, Africa and Asia, the night of November 24 is the better one.

Why your birth time is not optional

The rising sign — your ascendant — changes roughly every two hours, because it is simply the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment you were born. Get the time wrong by three hours and the ascendant, the house placements, and therefore most of the reading, are wrong too.

If it is genuinely lost, ask the reader for a solar chart (houses set by the Sun's position) rather than paying for rectification. Be sceptical of anyone who charges extra to "recover" your time.

What a real reading should cover

  • The big three: Sun (core drive), Moon (emotional wiring), Ascendant (how you come across).
  • House placements — which life areas the planets activate. This is where generic readings fall apart.
  • Aspects — the angles between planets; the conjunctions, squares and trines that describe tension and ease.
  • At least one specific application — career, relationships, or the current transits affecting you now.
  • A file you keep: chart image, PDF, or recording, plus a written summary.

Prices: what's fair in 2026

  • $10–$20 — written report, usually software-generated with a short human note.
  • $20–$60 — human-interpreted natal reading, 15–40 minutes recorded or a detailed PDF. The value band.
  • $60–$120 — natal plus transits or a year ahead, sometimes with a follow-up window.
  • $120+ — synastry (two charts), relocation charts, or ongoing work with a practitioner.

If a $12 gig promises "full natal chart with all aspects and houses explained", the economics are telling you it is a template. That is fine if you know that is what you are buying — the problem is only when it is sold as personal interpretation.

How to vet the seller

  • Ask what software or method they use and whether the chart image is included. A real astrologer answers in one line.
  • Ask what they need from you. If they do not ask for birth time, they are not calculating houses — full stop.
  • Check reviews for specifics: "she explained my 12th-house stellium and why work feels hidden" is a real review.
  • Look for transit work in the packages — applying the chart to now is the hard part, and it is what separates practitioners from hobbyists.

What if I don't know my birth time?

Ask for a solar chart, which sets houses by the Sun's position and does not need a time. Be wary of "rectification" upsells — recovering an unknown birth time is not something a reader can reliably do from your life events.

Is a birth chart the same as a horoscope?

No. A horoscope is written for a sun sign — one twelfth of the population. A natal chart is calculated for one exact moment and place, and it is unique to you.

What's the difference between natal, transit and synastry readings?

Natal is the chart you are born with. Transits compare today's sky to that chart — what is being activated now. Synastry overlays two people's charts, which is why it costs more.

How long should a good reading take?

20–40 minutes of content for a natal chart, or a well-structured PDF of a few thousand words. Much shorter than that and whole areas are being skipped.

Can astrology tell me when to change jobs or get married?

A reader can describe the character of a period — what kind of pressure or opening a transit tends to bring. Turning that into "quit on 14 March" is not something a responsible astrologer will do.

Written report or live session?

These are different products and the price gap between them is usually justified. A written report is researched in advance, longer, and yours to keep — better for a first chart, because you will want to re-read it as the terminology becomes familiar. A live session answers your actual questions, which matters when you have one specific situation rather than a general curiosity.

If the budget only covers one, take the written report first and spend the money on a session later, once you know which questions to ask. Going in cold to a live session wastes most of the time on basics you could have read.

Houses: the part most cheap readings skip

Planets in signs tell you how something operates. Houses tell you where in your life it operates — work, family, partnership, health, and so on. A reading without houses can tell you that you have a demanding Mars; a reading with houses can say which part of your life it is demanding in.

This is also the part that depends entirely on an accurate birth time, since houses rotate through the day. If a seller offers a full chart reading without asking for your time of birth, they are either estimating or skipping houses — and it is worth asking which.

Transits and timing: what a chart can legitimately say about 2026

Transits are simply where the planets are now, compared against where they were when you were born. That comparison is arithmetic, it is checkable, and it is the part of astrology that most resembles a method rather than a description.

What it supports is a statement about emphasis: a period in which a particular theme in your chart is being activated. What it does not support is a promise that a specific event will occur. A reader who says “this is a year the chart emphasises partnership questions” is working inside the method. One who says “you will meet someone in March” is not.

Synastry: adding a second chart

Synastry compares two charts by laying one over the other and examining the aspects between them — your Mars against their Venus, and so on. It is the most requested add-on to a birth chart reading and the one where the marketing most often outruns the method.

What it produces is a description of friction and ease between two patterns. That can be a genuinely useful way to talk about a relationship. What it cannot do is tell you whether the relationship will last, or whether a specific person is right for you. A reader who offers that verdict is selling certainty the method does not contain.

Red flags specific to chart readings

Beyond the general ones: a reading that does not ask for your birth time or place; one that reports your sun sign incorrectly — which you can check free in seconds; and one that treats your chart as a set of fixed predictions rather than a description of patterns.

The most expensive version of this is the “remedy” upsell: the chart reveals an affliction, a dosha, or a difficult placement, and a further purchase — a gemstone, a ritual, a clearing — resolves it. That structure is documented in consumer-protection guidance on psychic fraud, and it is the reason to walk away rather than negotiate.

The real difference between a marketplace like Fiverr and dedicated psychic networks like Keen and Kasamba is not quality — it is how you pay. One sells a defined piece of work at a fixed price; the others sell minutes. That single design choice changes what a reading feels like, what it costs, and what happens when it goes badly.

Where the fixed-price model wins

  • You cannot be surprised by the bill. The amount is agreed before you pay, which removes the thing people complain about most on per-minute networks.
  • You get a file. Most Fiverr readings arrive as a recording or document you can return to — a live call leaves you only memory and notes.
  • Asynchronous suits real life. No scheduling, no time zones, no watching the clock.
  • Scope is written down. Number of questions and format are part of the listing, which makes a dispute resolvable.

Where the per-minute model wins

  • Follow-up questions. Live means you can push back: "no, that is not quite it — let me rephrase."
  • Reading the person. Some people want the interaction itself, and a video call gives you cues a PDF never will.
  • A relationship over time. Networks make it easy to return to the same reader repeatedly.
The honest split: if your question can be written down clearly, buy a fixed-price reading. If you do not know your question yet and need to talk it through, a live network is worth the metered cost.

What to watch for on either model

  • Intro offers that convert to a subscription. On networks, check whether the discounted rate auto-renews.
  • "Only a few minutes more." Live readers sometimes extend sessions; set a budget before you start and hold it.
  • Upsell ladders. On marketplaces, watch for package tiers that sell "cleansing" or "block removal" rather than more reading time. Both patterns are covered in our red flags guide.
  • Off-platform payments. On any platform, a seller asking for a direct transfer is removing your protection.

Is one model more accurate than the other?

There is no evidence that payment structure affects accuracy. What it affects is how much control you have over cost and format — and that is the basis on which you should choose.

Which is cheaper for a single question?

Usually a fixed-price gig, because you are paying for a defined answer rather than for however long the conversation lasts. A live call that runs long is the most expensive way to ask one question.

Are the intro offers on psychic networks worth it?

They can be a cheap way to try a reader — but check the terms for auto-renewal and per-minute rates that apply afterwards, which are often far higher than the introductory price.

Can I use both?

Many people do: a marketplace reading for a recorded, considered answer, and a live network session when they want to talk something through. There is no loyalty owed to either model.

Which is safer if something goes wrong?

Fixed-price marketplaces, generally, because the scope is written down and payment is held until delivery. Per-minute networks handle disputes at their discretion, and refunds beyond credits are uncommon.
